### Users

Theater and arts people in and around Los Angeles: audiences of past shows, friends of the
company, people who might come to a drum circle or an acting class. They arrive mostly from
Instagram, often on a phone, to see what Takes a Village is, what is coming next, and what it has
done. The jobs: find out what the company does, see the year's work, follow on Instagram, donate,
and (once the Offerings page exists) ask about drum circles and classes.

### Brand Personality

Takes a Village LA is a small nonprofit theater company: plays in a Van Nuys backyard, drum
circles, arts workshops. Three words: handmade, warm, theatrical. The voice is a script's: stage
directions, acts and scenes, ticket stubs, "Hello, Friends!". Friendly and plain, never
corporate, never salesy.

### Aesthetic Direction

- Very artsy. Paper, tape and snapshots: taped photos, torn paper edges, slight tilts, ticket
  stubs. Nick likes the tape on the Constellations flier; reuse that tape.
- Light theme on cream and warm paper. Palette in `:root` of `index.html`: teal `#2EADA3`,
  orange `#E8721A`, brown `#5C3317`, cream `#FFF6E8`, warm `#FDE8C0`, text `#3A1F08`. For text
  use the ink versions (`--ink #4A2811`, `--teal-ink #176F67`, `--orange-ink #B4530C`).
- Type: Fraunces (italic) for section headings and display lines, Nunito for body text, Baloo 2
  only where it echoes the logo (decided 2026-11-24).
- Decided 2026-11-24: the show section moves off navy onto paper, with the taped flier as the
  only dark object, and the glowing title goes.
- Anti-references: dark "tech" sections, neon glows, glassmorphism, gradient text, generic SaaS
  landing-page cards and icon grids, stock photos. Anything that looks templated.
- Not to change (Nick, "Decided, no change"): the Donate button as it is, both "Hello, Friends!"
  headings, both back-to-top controls.

### Design Principles

1. One visual language all the way down: every section belongs to the paper-and-tape world.
2. Real photos of real people from the company's own events, never stock.
3. Say plainly what the company does and what is next; the theatrical voice decorates, it does
   not hide the facts.
4. Readable for everyone: WCAG AA contrast (checked with `docs/measure-contrast.js`), works at
   390px wide, motion only under `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`.
5. Static and simple: one HTML page with inline CSS, no build, JavaScript only if Nick agrees.
